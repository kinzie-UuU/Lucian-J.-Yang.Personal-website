# Portfolio Taxonomy

Version: v0.1  
Status: working synthesis after 100-site first-pass audit

## Purpose

This document compresses the first-pass audit of 100 portfolio and studio websites into a smaller set of reusable families.

It is not a list of "best looking websites".

It is a design-operating model for answering:

- what kinds of portfolio sites actually recur
- what design logic makes them work
- which parts are reusable
- which kinds are appropriate for Kinzie
- which kinds should later become skills

## Important Boundary

The 100 audited sites do **not** represent one homogeneous category.

They include:

- solo creative portfolios
- creative developer portfolios
- boutique agencies
- global agencies
- production companies
- post-production houses
- immersive-tech studios
- web3 / niche technical studios

This means the right output is **not** "100 references -> 100 styles".

The right output is:

- a smaller number of stable portfolio families
- decision rules that choose among them
- a rejection system that prevents identity mismatch

## The 8 Core Families

## 1. Strategic Authority Portfolio

### Core pattern

These sites establish authority through:

- clear positioning
- strong point of view
- direct strategic language
- proof-rich case framing

They often feel premium without needing extreme interaction.

### Typical signals

- strong statement near the top
- explicit proposition
- case studies tied to outcomes or decisions
- bold typography
- restrained motion

### Best represented by

- Ragged Edge
- Portal One
- Bulletproof
- Brand Appart
- selected parts of Your Creative

### Why it works

It makes the visitor understand:

- who you are
- why your judgment matters
- what kind of work you should be hired for

### Risks

- can become too agency-generic
- can feel too corporate if the typography lacks personality

### Kinzie fit

`very high`

This is one of the most important families for Kinzie because it naturally supports:

- judgment
- positioning
- clarity
- commercial trust

## 2. Premium Restraint Portfolio

### Core pattern

These sites look expensive because they are:

- sparse
- image-led or type-led
- carefully paced
- structurally quiet

They rely on confidence, not noise.

### Typical signals

- low-UI first screen
- calm project browsing
- generous whitespace
- refined image trust
- minimal but exact typography

### Best represented by

- Jens Bosman
- Twice
- Lazy Eight
- selected parts of Sergey Lisovskiy

### Why it works

The site feels authored and mature because nothing is trying too hard.

### Risks

- collapses if the work is weak
- can become anonymous if the identity statement is too quiet

### Kinzie fit

`very high`

Kinzie can borrow this family heavily for:

- homepage restraint
- layout discipline
- project-entry confidence

## 3. Archive Credibility Portfolio

### Core pattern

These sites establish trust through the visible weight of practice:

- many projects
- many clients
- many years
- clear field labels
- archive-like browsing

### Typical signals

- list or grid archive
- explicit years / roles / fields
- reduced decorative layer
- credibility from breadth and order

### Best represented by

- Sergey Lisovskiy
- selected parts of Cappen
- some agency and production company sites in the corpus

### Why it works

The visitor sees a sustained body of practice instead of a curated vibe only.

### Risks

- can become dry
- can look like a directory instead of a designed experience

### Kinzie fit

`high`

Very useful for later stages of Kinzie, especially if the project archive grows and needs to feel substantial.

## 4. Concept-Coherent Studio

### Core pattern

These sites choose one controlling metaphor or concept system and let it shape:

- copy
- process framing
- CTA language
- section transitions
- atmosphere

The best ones are not just visually themed. The concept shapes the information behavior too.

### Typical signals

- repeated conceptual vocabulary
- strong framing device
- process language aligned with the metaphor
- site identity feels unified

### Best represented by

- Double Play
- Orage Studio
- some atmosphere-heavy boutique studios in the list

### Why it works

It gives the site memorability and coherence.

### Risks

- if the concept is too cute, the site can feel gimmicky
- if the concept does not fit the professional identity, it weakens trust

### Kinzie fit

`medium to high`

Very useful if translated into packaging-native logic:

- system
- sequence
- reveal
- comparison
- material judgment

Not useful if it becomes decorative metaphor only.

## 5. Image-Led Production / Film Portfolio

### Core pattern

These sites assume the media is already powerful enough to carry the experience.

The interface stays light because:

- the stills or films are strong
- the category system is enough
- the emotional tone comes from the content

### Typical signals

- sparse navigation
- category-led work browsing
- large stills or featured media
- low explanatory friction

### Best represented by

- Twice
- Robot
- film and production studios across the corpus

### Why it works

When the media is excellent, heavy UI is unnecessary.

### Risks

- fails completely if the media quality is not elite
- can undersell service logic

### Kinzie fit

`medium`

Good as a lesson in restraint and trust, but Kinzie cannot rely on this family alone because packaging work often needs more explanation than films do.

## 6. Hybrid Solo Studio

### Core pattern

One-person or very small studios that need to feel:

- capable
- complete
- professional
- not inflated

These sites are especially important because they show how a solo practice can avoid looking small.

### Typical signals

- clear role stacking
- selected work first
- explicit capabilities
- compact personal intro
- controlled proof of breadth

### Best represented by

- Karim Saab
- RAYRAYlab
- Clay Boan
- selected solo studio sites in the list

### Why it works

It organizes multi-capability identity without pretending to be a giant agency.

### Risks

- can drift into "creative developer portfolio" identity too easily
- can become capability soup if not sharply edited

### Kinzie fit

`very high`

This is a core family for Kinzie because the site needs to feel like:

- one person or small practice
- but with strategic range and real-world execution strength

## 7. Creative Developer / Pure Frontend Showcase

### Core pattern

These sites make technical craft part of the identity itself.

The site says:

- I do not only have taste
- I can control the medium directly

### Typical signals

- SVG deformation
- pseudo-3D fields
- transform-based depth
- custom scroll and hover logic
- strong interaction signature

### Best represented by

- Antoine Wodniack
- Jordan Delcros
- Stas Bondar
- other creative-developer entries in the corpus

### Why it works

It turns the website into direct proof of implementation talent.

### Risks

- identity mismatch for non-developer users
- maintenance complexity
- content readability drop
- spectacle can replace clarity

### Kinzie fit

`medium as inspiration, low as identity`

Kinzie should borrow:

- interaction grammar
- pseudo-3D discipline
- SVG / DOM craft

Kinzie should **not** borrow:

- developer self-positioning
- over-technical identity signals

## 8. Immersive Tech / Frontier Studio

### Core pattern

These sites use advanced digital environments as proof:

- WebGL
- real-time 3D
- WebGPU
- immersive digital worlds

### Typical signals

- environment-first presentation
- future-tech language
- high spectacle
- interactive systems as the main attraction

### Best represented by

- Alche
- Utsubo
- Immersive Garden
- dverso
- other metaverse / frontier-tech entries

### Why it works

The studio's value is precisely in creating advanced digital environments.

### Risks

- category mismatch
- extremely high complexity
- weak transferability to more grounded professional identities

### Kinzie fit

`low`

Useful mainly as a boundary reference:

- what not to over-borrow
- where the line of "too technical for Kinzie" sits

## Cross-Family Dimensions

The 8 families above can also be read across five reusable dimensions.

## A. Visual Thesis

Common recurring visual theses:

- strategic typographic confidence
- premium calm restraint
- image-led cinematic authority
- pseudo-3D spatial field
- interface fiction
- atmosphere-heavy boutique mood

## B. Interaction Thesis

Common recurring interaction theses:

- scroll narrative
- hover emphasis
- category partitioning
- pseudo-3D field movement
- concept-driven section transitions
- media-led stillness

## C. Content Architecture

Common recurring architecture patterns:

- statement -> works -> method -> contact
- works-first homepage
- archive-first homepage
- category split homepage
- proposition -> services -> proof -> CTA

## D. Material System

Common recurring material systems:

- black / white contrast
- polished image-led surfaces
- restrained grain / texture
- premium typography over decoration
- dark cinematic planes
- sparse interfaces with strong media

## E. Trust Mechanism

How these sites create trust:

- proof of strategic thinking
- proof of large practice archive
- proof of technical execution
- proof of visual quality
- proof of niche specialization

This dimension is especially important for later decision rules.

## Kinzie Mapping

Kinzie should not try to become a generic "creative portfolio".

The best composite direction for Kinzie is:

### Primary families

- Strategic Authority Portfolio
- Premium Restraint Portfolio
- Hybrid Solo Studio

### Secondary supporting families

- Archive Credibility Portfolio
- Concept-Coherent Studio
- selective parts of Creative Developer / Pure Frontend Showcase

### Families to mostly reject

- Immersive Tech / Frontier Studio
- fully image-led production/fashion logic as the main structure

## What Kinzie Should Borrow

- clear homepage proposition
- strategic language around judgment
- restrained project-entry structure
- archive confidence
- one-person studio clarity
- selective SVG / DOM-based interaction craft

## What Kinzie Should Avoid

- over-gamified navigation
- developer-coded identity
- WebGL-heavy hero systems by default
- portfolio structures that hide content behind pure spectacle
- relying only on imagery without strategic explanation

## Early Skill Candidate Directions

These families strongly suggest the next stage should not be one monolithic skill.

Promising candidate families:

- `portfolio-strategic-authority`
- `portfolio-premium-restraint`
- `portfolio-hybrid-solo-studio`
- `portfolio-archive-credibility`
- `portfolio-concept-coherence`
- `portfolio-pure-frontend-interaction`
- `portfolio-frontier-boundary-filter`

And one orchestrator above them:

- `portfolio-style-engine`

## What This Taxonomy Changes

Before the audit, the reference pool looked like a mass of unrelated inspiration.

After the first-pass audit, the picture is much clearer:

- the sites repeat stable professional archetypes
- the differences are mostly about positioning and degree, not endless novelty
- the most important design skill is selection, not imitation

That means the next step is not more browsing.

The next step is:

1. stabilize this taxonomy
2. define skill candidates
3. create a Kinzie-specific selection layer
4. then connect the system to generation workflows
